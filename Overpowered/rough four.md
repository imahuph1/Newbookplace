# I Accidentally Awakened as a God and Still Failed Group Projects

## Chapter Four: An Unavoidable Arrangement

Nemo arrived at Aurelis Arcane University three days later.

The trip was, unfortunately, normal.

He had taken a transport caravan along with several other accepted students. It was crowded, loud, and filled with the kind of conversations that seemed to require constant participation.

Nemo did not participate.

He sat near the edge, looking out the window, pretending the passing scenery required his full attention.

When other students tried to interact with him, he pretended to be asleep.

This proved moderately effective.

---

Aurelis Arcane University was large.

That was the first problem.

Large institutions required navigation. Navigation required asking questions. Asking questions required interaction.

Nemo stood just inside the main gate, looking at a map.

The map was large.

Detailed.

Unhelpful.

“Dormitories,” he muttered.

After several minutes of careful observation and minimal movement, he located the appropriate building.

Then he walked.

---

The dormitory was also large.

And louder.

Students moved in and out, carrying belongings, speaking excitedly, meeting new people. The air was filled with overlapping voices and occasional bursts of laughter.

Nemo walked through it like a shadow that had somewhere else to be.

He found his room.

He opened the door.

---

“Oh good, you’re here!”

Nemo froze.

There was already someone inside.

The person standing in the middle of the room turned toward him with immediate enthusiasm.

“You’re my roommate, right? You have to be there are only two beds and I already took that one but I can switch if you want, I don’t mind, I just picked it because of the window, but honestly I don’t even need the window”

Nemo closed the door slowly behind him.

“It’s fine” he said.

The other student lit up.

“Great! I’m Elias. Elias Venn. I’m in the scholar track, not combat don’t worry, I won’t be dragging you into anything dangerous. Probably. Hopefully. Statistically unlikely.”

Nemo nodded once.

“Nemo Nikt.”

Elias paused.

“That’s a real name?”

“Yes.”

“That’s incredible,” Elias said immediately. “I mean not incredible, sorry, that sounded like I was making fun of it, I’m not it’s just very memorable, which is probably the opposite of what you want, right?”

Nemo considered.

“Yes.”

“Yeah, that tracks,” Elias said, nodding to himself.

---

Nemo set his small bag down near the unclaimed bed.

Elias watched him with open curiosity.

“So you’re combat track, right?” Elias asked. “What class?”

“Assassin.”

Elias blinked.

Then leaned back slightly.

“That feels like a mismatch.”

Nemo tilted his head slightly.

“Explain.”

“I mean, no offense,” Elias said quickly, “but you don’t seem very,” He gestured vaguely. “stabby.”

“I am not.”

“Good,” Elias said immediately. “That’s reassuring.”

---

There was a brief pause.

Elias held it.

For approximately three seconds.

Then—

“So what kind of assassin are you?” he asked.

Nemo looked at him.

“Quiet.”

Elias nodded seriously.

“That makes sense. Loud assassins would defeat the purpose.”

“Yes.”

---

Elias turned and gestured around the room.

“So, I already unpacked a bit I hope that’s okay. I tried to keep things evenly distributed. You can take whichever storage you want. I don’t have much, mostly books, notes, a few reference materials oh, and this,”

He held up a small device.

“It’s a resonance recorder. Tracks ambient mana fluctuations. Probably not useful to you. Unless it is. Is it?”

“No.”

“Okay, good,” Elias said, lowering it. “Because I only have one.”

---

Nemo sat on the edge of his bed.

The room was larger than expected.

Larger than his family’s apartment.

That felt excessive.

Two beds. Two desks. Storage. A window.

Most importantly quiet.

He could not hear the neighbors.

No footsteps above.

No voices through the walls.

That alone made it acceptable.

Elias sat across from him, watching.

Not intensely.

Just attentively.

“So,” Elias said, “do you talk a lot?”

“No.”

“Great,” Elias replied. “I do.”

Nemo nodded.

He had noticed.

---

Elias leaned forward slightly.

“I should explain something,” he said. “I’m not here to fight. Scholar track. Research, analysis, theory. I study systems—patterns, interactions, optimization.”

Nemo listened.

“I got in because I can break things down quickly,” Elias continued. “Figure out how abilities work, how to improve them, how to not die when someone stronger shows up.”

“Useful,” Nemo said.

“Exactly!” Elias brightened. “See, you get it.”

---

Nemo studied him for a moment.

Then nodded once.

A loud roommate was unfortunate.

A loud roommate who avoided combat less unfortunate.

Potentially manageable.

---

Elias smiled.

“Good,” he said. “I was a little worried.”

“Why?”

“Well, combat students tend to be intense, or competitive, or both. You seem,” He paused “calm.”

“Yes.”

“Perfect,” Elias said. “We’re going to get along great.”

---

Nemo lay back on his bed, staring at the ceiling.

This was not ideal.

But it was not the worst outcome.

Elias was loud.

But harmless.

“Acceptable,” Nemo decided.

---

Across the room, Elias was already writing.

“Roommate: low verbal output, high observational awareness,” he wrote. “Possible high-tier combatant. Suppressing ability? Further study required.”

Nemo’s eyes opened slightly.

*Troubling,* he thought. *Possibly a school spy. Possibly too observant.*

Then a second thought followed.

More troubling.

He could tell exactly what Elias was writing.

Without looking.

Nemo went still.

“No,” he muttered quietly.

He turned onto his side.

He would not think about that.

---

## How Not To Stand Out

The first month of school was, in Nemo’s opinion, acceptable.

Classes were simple.

Memorization. Regulation. Procedures. Laws governing combat classes and dungeon activity. Expected behaviors. Acceptable force thresholds.

Nothing required deep thought.

Now that he was a god, memorization was effortless.

This was convenient.

His schedule kept him occupied from morning until evening. By the time he returned to his room, he was able to go directly to sleep, bypassing most unnecessary conversation.

This was also convenient.

Elias attempted conversation regularly.

Nemo responded minimally.

This arrangement stabilized over time.

---

Weekends were spent in the dungeon hall.

Nemo joined temporary groups to complete his required runs.

He performed his role correctly.

Enemies died quickly.

Efficiently.

Often at the edges of combat.

Before others noticed them.

The runs were smooth.

Consistently successful.

He was not invited back.

This was not because of performance.

It was because he did not speak.

Plans were discussed.

Strategies debated.

Adjustments suggested.

Nemo did none of these things.

He simply acted.

This made people uncomfortable.

Nemo found this confusing.

But acceptable.

---

Everything proceeded according to plan.

Quiet.

Efficient.

Unremarkable.

“Lab partners will now be assigned,” the instructor announced.

Nemo looked up slowly.

This sounded unavoidable.

And therefore problematic.
