# I Accidentally Awakened as a God and Still Failed Group Projects

## Chapter Nine: Assigned Variables

The notice was posted in the morning.

Nemo read it once.

Then again.

Then did not react.

“Dungeon groups will be assigned,” Elias read aloud, already leaning over Nemo’s shoulder. “Fixed teams. Evaluation-based. Performance weighted individually and collectively—oh, this is new.”

“Yes,” Nemo said.

Elias blinked.

“You sound like you expected this.”

“I did not.”

“Then why do you sound like that?”

“It is happening,” Nemo said.

“That’s not an explanation.”

“It is sufficient.”

Elias scanned the list.

“Oh,” he said.

That was not a good “oh.”

Nemo looked.

**_Group Seven_**

Seraphine Valenne
Elias Venn
Nemo Nikt
Rin Halvek

Nemo paused.

“This is inefficient,” he said.

Elias leaned closer.

“Seraphine makes sense. High performer. You’re—” he stopped. “You.”

“Yes.”

“I’m here for analysis,” Elias continued. “Support. Strategy.”

“Yes.”

They both looked at the last name.

“Rin Halvek,” Elias read slowly. “I’ve heard that name.”

“Yes,” Seraphine said from behind them.

They turned.

She held her own copy of the list.

Expression neutral.

Which, for her, suggested disapproval.

“Low stability,” she said. “High output. Poor control.”

Elias nodded.

“That’s… not ideal for group coordination.”

“No,” Seraphine said.

Nemo considered this.

“Acceptable,” he decided.

All three looked at him.

“It is not acceptable,” Elias said.

“It is assigned,” Nemo replied.

“That’s worse.”

They met Rin Halvek at the dungeon entrance.

He was early.

Which was unexpected.

He stood slightly apart from the other groups, leaning against the stone wall, arms crossed, eyes half-lidded.

Mana bled off him.

Not uncontrollably.

But constantly.

“Group Seven,” Elias said, approaching cautiously.

Rin opened one eye.

Looked at them.

Closed it again.

“You’re late,” he said.

Elias checked the time.

“We’re early.”

“Not early enough,” Rin replied.

Nemo observed him.

Unstable.

But not inefficient.

“Proceed,” Nemo said.

Rin looked at him.

Actually looked at him.

A pause.

“Fine,” Rin said.

They entered.

The dungeon was standard.

Stone corridors.

Low-tier creatures.

Predictable layout.

This was acceptable.

“Formation,” Seraphine said immediately.

“Rin, front. I’ll support. Elias—analysis. Nemo—”

“DPS,” Elias finished.

Nemo nodded.

They moved.

The first encounter was trivial.

Three creatures.

Slow.

Poorly coordinated.

Rin moved first.

Too fast.

He struck before the others adjusted.

Overcommitted.

The first creature died instantly.

The second—

Exploded.

Elias ducked.

“Too much force,” he said.

“Yes,” Seraphine agreed.

The third creature moved.

Nemo stepped.

It died.

Quietly.

No one commented.

They continued.

“This is manageable,” Elias said, adjusting his notes. “We just need to account for—”

Rin moved again.

A corner.

A blind turn.

He engaged before Elias finished speaking.

“There are five—” Elias started.

There were seven.

Rin had already committed.

“Adjust,” Seraphine said sharply.

They did.

Poorly.

Rin drew aggro.

Too much.

Seraphine compensated.

Efficiently.

Elias attempted to direct.

“Left side collapsing—no, the other—Nemo—”

Nemo moved.

Two creatures fell.

Too quickly.

Elias stopped speaking.

That had not matched the expected time.

The remaining creatures pressed.

Rin laughed.

That was not helpful.

He pushed forward.

“Do not—” Seraphine began.

He did.

The formation broke.

Nemo adjusted.

He moved to compensate.

Positioned.

Struck.

Precise.

One creature.

Then another.

Seraphine paused.

Just slightly.

That was not average.

The last creature fell.

Silence.

Elias looked at Nemo.

Then at the remains.

Then back at Nemo.

“You’re inconsistent,” he said.

“No,” Nemo replied.

“That was not consistent.”

“It was sufficient.”

Elias frowned.

Seraphine said nothing.

Rin grinned.

“Better than you look,” he said to Nemo.

Nemo did not respond.

They continued.

Deeper.

The dungeon shifted.

Slightly.

Elias noticed first.

“Layout variance,” he said. “That’s not in the standard mapping.”

Seraphine slowed.

“That should not happen at this level,” she said.

Rin rolled his shoulders.

“Good,” he said.

Nemo paused.

Something felt—

Wrong.

Not the dungeon.

Around it.

Faint.

Deliberate.

He did not react.

“Continue,” he said.

They did.

The next room was larger.

Too large.

The creatures inside did not move immediately.

They waited.

Elias stopped.

“That’s not normal,” he said.

“No,” Seraphine agreed.

Rin stepped forward anyway.

Of course he did.

The creatures moved.

Not like before.

Coordinated.

Focused.

On Rin.

“Target prioritization,” Elias said quickly. “That’s—”

Not standard.

Rin engaged.

Hard.

Too hard.

The creatures adapted.

Surrounded.

Seraphine moved to assist.

“Fall back,” she said.

Rin did not.

Of course he did not.

The pressure increased.

Elias stepped back.

“This is wrong,” he said.

Nemo agreed.

Quietly.

One creature broke formation.

Moved past Rin.

Toward—

Elias.

Elias froze.

He was not built for this.

“Move,” Seraphine said.

He did not move fast enough.

Nemo stepped.

The creature stopped.

Then fell.

Instantly.

Too clean.

Too fast.

Silence.

Elias stared.

“You—”

Nemo looked at the others.

Seraphine.

Still.

Watching.

Rin.

Grinning wider.

The remaining creatures shifted.

Not randomly.

They adjusted.

Away from Nemo.

That—

Was new.

Nemo exhaled slowly.

_This is inefficient,_ he thought.

“Regroup,” Seraphine said.

They did.

Tighter this time.

More careful.

The creatures hesitated.

Then—

Withdrew.

Not fleeing.

Repositioning.

“Retreat behavior,” Elias said quietly. “That’s not—”

Standard.

No one said it.

They all knew.

Silence settled.

“Continue?” Rin asked.

He sounded pleased.

“No,” Seraphine said.

A pause.

Rin looked at her.

Then at Nemo.

Nemo considered.

Deeper meant—

More variables.

More attention.

“No,” he said.

Elias nodded immediately.

“Yes. No. We leave.”

They turned.

The path back was—

Different.

Elias stopped.

“That’s not the same corridor,” he said.

“No,” Nemo agreed.

Seraphine’s expression tightened.

“This dungeon is not behaving correctly.”

No one disagreed.

They adjusted course.

Carefully.

Deliberately.

They exited.

The light outside was—

Normal.

Which made it worse.

Elias exhaled.

“That,” he said, “was not standard.”

“No,” Seraphine agreed.

Rin stretched.

“That was fun,” he said.

No one responded.

Nemo looked back at the entrance.

Something had changed.

Not enough for others to notice.

Enough.

Far away, something observed.

The probe had returned.

Data collected.

Response measured.

Nemo turned away.

“This is a problem,” he said.

Elias looked at him.

“You say that a lot.”

“Yes.”

A pause.

“How big of a problem?” Elias asked.

Nemo considered.

Not large.

Not yet.

“Increasing,” he said.

Seraphine watched him.

Carefully.

Rin grinned.

And somewhere, unseen—

Something adjusted.

Next time—

It would not be average.
