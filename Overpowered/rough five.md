# I Accidentally Awakened as a God and Still Failed Group Projects

## Chapter Five: Inconvenient Suitor

“Lab partners will now be assigned.”

Nemo felt a quiet sense of inevitability settle in.

This was, statistically, the worst possible outcome.

He sat still as names were called, students shifting around him, some pleased, others less so. Conversations started immediately—introductions, negotiations, mild complaints.

Nemo did none of these things.

He waited.

“Elias Venn”

Elias perked up immediately from across the room.

“Yes, that’s me.”

“paired with”

Nemo stopped listening.

That was not his problem.

"Next Nemo Nikt, paird with Seraphine Vale."

Elias blinked classmates made sounds of jealousy Nemo simply nodded.

The instructor did not pause.

“Next” they continued.

---

The alchemy lab was quiet.

This was unexpected.

There were no weapons. No shouting. No immediate danger. Just rows of workstations, neatly arranged tools, and shelves filled with labeled materials.

Nemo approved.

Elias did not.

“This is incredible,” Elias whispered. “Do you see the setup? The organization? The material density alone suggests—”

“Yes,” Nemo said.

Elias stopped.

“You don’t care, do you?”

“No.”

“Right,” Elias said. “That tracks.”

---

Elias went to his station when Nemos partner entered the lab room

The shift in atmosphere was immediate.

Students nearby grew quieter.

Subtly.

Respectfully.

Nemo looked up.

A girl stood there—well-dressed, composed, with the kind of posture that suggested she had never once questioned whether she belonged somewhere.

“Excuse me,” she said, her tone polite, measured “I am assigned to this station.”

Nemo glanced at the sheet.

“Yes.”

Seraphine followed his gaze.

There was a brief pause.

“I see,” she said.

Another pause.

Then, with a small exhale "Are you always like this?"

"Yes."

---

She set her materials down.

Gracefully.

Deliberately.

Elias watched this entire exchange with growing fascination.

“This is new,” he muttered.

---

They had not worked together for more than five minutes before the second interruption arrived.

This one was louder.

“Seraphine.”

The name was spoken with familiarity.

Possession, almost.

Nemo did not look up immediately.

He continued measuring out a reagent.

Accurately.

Precisely.

“Seraphine,” the voice repeated, sharper now.

Nemo finished measuring.

Then looked up.

A young man stood at the edge of their station, dressed in polished attire, posture rigid, expression carefully controlled in the way of someone who expected to be obeyed.

He was not pleased.

“You’ve been assigned incorrectly,” he said.

Seraphine did not look at him.

“I have not.”

His jaw tightened slightly.

“This is beneath you.”

“That is not your decision.”

The silence that followed was brief.

Tense.

Then his attention shifted.

To Nemo.

“Drop the class,” he said.

Nemo blinked.

“No.”

The young man frowned.

“You don’t understand.”

Nemo simply shrugged, the statement was nonsensical.

Elias very slowly stepped backward.

“You will withdraw,” the noble continued, voice lowering. “Or there will be consequences.”

Nemo considered that.

“What consequences?”

The noble paused.

Just slightly.

“You’ll find out.”

Nemo nodded once.

“No.”

---

Over the next few weeks, Nemo did, in fact, find out.

Or rather he noticed that things were happening.

Equipment went missing.

Then reappeared.

Materials were mislabeled with amateur labels, it was almost amusing.

Group assignments shifted in subtle ways that made tasks slightly less efficient.

Students whispered occasionally, not near him about him.

Nemo observed all of this with mild interest.

It did not significantly impact his routine.

He completed his work returned to his room attended his classes completed his dungeon runs.

Everything remained tolerable.

---

The noble, however, grew increasingly frustrated.

Each attempt at intimidation resulted in the same outcome.

Minimal response no escalation no visible concern.

“You think this is amusing?” he demanded one afternoon.

“No.” Nemo said.

“You are being deliberately difficult.”

“No.”

“You are ignoring me.”

“Yes.”

The noble stopped.

“That was not an acceptable answer.”

Nemo considered this shrugged and went back to work.

---

Elias, who had been present for several of these exchanges, had taken to writing them down.

“Fascinating,” he murmured once. “Complete failure of expected social pressure response.”

“Yes” Nemo said.

“I don’t think that’s a good thing.”

“It is efficient.”

---

Eventually, something changed.

The noble approached again.

More controlled this time.

More formal.

“You’ve made a mistake,” he said.

Nemo looked at him shrugged.

“Good,” the noble said. “Then you understand.”

“No.”

A pause.

“You lack refinement,” the noble said, voice tightening. “You lack awareness. You lack the basic understanding of where you stand.”

Nemo considered this carefully.

Then spoke.

“You are inefficient.”

The silence that followed was absolute.

Elias stopped writing.

Seraphine looked up.

The noble stared at him.

“What did you say?”

“You are inefficient,” Nemo repeated. “Your actions have not produced results.”

That had not been intended as an insult.

It was simply an observation.

Unfortunately it qualified as an insult.

---

“I challenge you,” the noble said, voice sharp. “Duel. Three days. This weekend.”

Nemo sighed internally.

“Do you accept?”

“Yes,” Nemo said.

Because declining would create more problems.

---

That evening, Nemo sat in his room.

Elias was pacing.

“This is bad,” he said. “This is very bad. You cannot duel him. Do you know who that is? Do you understand what kind of situation this is?”

“No.”

Elias stopped.

“That’s worse.”

---

Nemo lay back on his bed, staring at the ceiling.

Three days.

A public duel.

Attention expectations escalation all of it unnecessary.

If he wins the duel he still looses.

There was a better solution.

---

That night, Nemo focused.

Carefully, reluctantly.

He reached for something he had been deliberately avoiding.

Secrets they came easily.

The noble his family information layered on information.

Tax evasion embezzlement bribes.

Quiet meetings with groups that should not exist.

Beast cult affiliations.

Documented, recorded, hidden, until now.

Nemo exhaled slowly.

---

The next morning documents appeared on desks across the country.

Local reporters, national agencies, law enforcement.

Evidence, copies of ledgers, video and audio recordings.

Delivered without explanation.

Enough evidence to bring the family name into doubt, to destroy their credibility, for investigations to start, both overtly and covertly.

---

The noble did not attend class the next day.

One day later, his name was removed from the rolls.

There were rumors as to why, nothing official was ever stated.  The Vales, the rumors said, crossed someone they should not have crossed.  Nobody thought of Nemo, he is just a student without backing.

---

Elias burst into their room that evening.

“You are not going to believe this,”

Nemo did not look up.

“The duel is canceled,” Elias continued. “He’s been pulled from the university. His entire family is under investigation this is massive, I mean *massive*—”

“Yes,” Nemo said.

Elias stopped.

“You already know.”

“Yes.”

“How?”

Nemo closed his eyes, "His name is no longer on the class roster."

"What do you mean, how do you know that?"

"The teacher did not call his name this morning."

---

The problem had been removed.

No duel, no attention, no escalation.

---

“Acceptable,” Nemo decided.

Though as he drifted toward sleep he made a quiet note.

Using that ability was still inconvenient.
