## Chapter Two: Divinity, Unfortunately

With his mind still unhelpfully active, Nemo dismissed the previous message and opened his status panel again—this time properly.

If this was going to ruin his life, he preferred to know the details.

---

> **Name:** Nemo Nikt
> **Age:** 16
> **Class:** God
> **Domain:** Shadows, Secrets, Assassination
> **Level:** 1
>
> **Divine Authority Granted**
>
> * All skills are compatible. Restrictions removed.
> * Perception of secrets available upon focus.
> * Authority over bloodlines unlocked.
> * Prayer reception enabled.
> * Shrine projection available.
>
> **Attributes:** D-10 (All)

---

Nemo read it once.

Then again.

Then a third time, slower, in case something improved on repetition.

It did not.

He focused on the last line.

“D-minus ten,” he said quietly.

That seemed… incorrect.

From what he remembered, attributes were usually represented as numbers. Strength, agility, endurance—measurable things. Not letters. Certainly not letters with numbers attached in a way that suggested someone had stopped explaining halfway through.

A normal person his age averaged around ten in most attributes.

This was not that.

He didn’t know exactly what D-10 meant, but it felt like the kind of thing that would accidentally break furniture.

Or people.

Neither outcome was particularly convenient.

He moved on.

“All skills are compatible,” he read.

That sounded inefficient from a design perspective. Restrictions existed for a reason. Removing them entirely suggested a lack of oversight, which did not inspire confidence.

“Perception of secrets…”

He paused.

Then, against his better judgment, looked up.

A woman passed by outside his room, her footsteps faint through the thin walls.

Nemo focused.

The information arrived immediately.

Not words. Not images. Just… knowing.

She had hidden money behind a loose panel near her kitchen sink.
Her son knew.
He hadn’t told her he knew.
He was waiting.

Nemo stopped focusing.

“…No,” he said.

That was not useful.

That was not something he wanted to have.

But the moment lingered longer than it should have.

Because it hadn’t just been those three facts.

It had been *more*.

Fragments. Connections. Hints of other things—conversations held behind closed doors, agreements made quietly, truths that existed specifically because no one else knew them.

And they were… there.

Not fully formed. Not actively present.

But accessible.

If he wanted to look.

Nemo went very still.

“…No,” he repeated, more firmly this time.

He did not want to look.

Knowing small things was already a problem. Knowing *everything* was—

Inconvenient.

Dangerous.

Unnecessary.

Enough secrets drifted at the edge of his awareness to ruin lives, collapse families, possibly destabilize entire sections of the city if handled incorrectly.

Possibly more.

He exhaled slowly and looked back at the panel.

“Authority over bloodlines.”

He stared at that line for several seconds.

Then deliberately looked away.

Some abilities did not need testing.

The wording alone was enough.

Destroy a bloodline. Elevate one. Alter something that extended far beyond a single person.

That was not a skill.

That was a mistake waiting to happen.

“Prayer reception enabled.”

Nemo hesitated.

Then, with the same careful lack of enthusiasm he applied to everything else, he reached for it.

Something opened.

The world didn’t change, but his awareness did—stretching outward, thin and wide, brushing against something vast and scattered.

Then the whispers began.

Not loud. Not overwhelming. Just… numerous.

—*hide me*
—*they can’t know*
—*just this once*
—*please*
—*make it quiet*

Each voice was small on its own.

Together, they were not.

Nemo shut it off immediately.

The silence that followed was immediate and complete, like a door slamming shut on the other side of reality.

He stood still for a moment.

“…Absolutely not.”

That was not a feature.

That was a problem.

He glanced at the final line.

“Shrine projection.”

He considered it briefly.

Speaking to people was already undesirable.

Speaking to people as a disembodied voice from a shrine—likely in moments of stress, desperation, or poor decision-making—seemed significantly worse.

There would be expectations.

Follow-up questions.

Possibly gratitude.

Nemo closed the panel.

Then opened it again.

Then closed it once more.

Nothing changed.

He exhaled slowly and lay back on his bed, staring at the ceiling.

Summary.

He could see things he should not see.
Hear things he did not want to hear.
Do things he had no intention of testing.

And, according to the system, his “D-10” attributes meant he was likely far stronger than anyone he would reasonably meet.

At Level 1.

“…This is excessive,” he said.

There was only one reasonable course of action.

He would ignore it.

Not the panel—that would be irresponsible.

But everything else.

No secret viewing. No prayer listening. No bloodline interference.

None of it.

He would proceed exactly as planned. Attend the entrance exam. Perform adequately. Remain unremarkable.

Live quietly.

It was, in his opinion, a very good plan.

---

Across the city, in a narrow alley between two older buildings, a small shrine sat half-forgotten. Dust had gathered at its base. The offerings were old, the candles burned low.

A figure knelt in front of it anyway.

They hesitated, then spoke.

“God of shadows… if you’re listening…”

Nemo turned onto his side, pulling the thin blanket up slightly.

“…I need help.”

Nemo did not respond.

More accurately—

He chose not to.

But the words did not disappear.

They lingered.

Faint. Distant. Persistent.

He frowned slightly into his pillow.

“…inconvenient,” he muttered.

---

It was early evening when his parents returned home.

Nemo was already in the main room, having prepared a simple meal. Nothing elaborate—just enough to be functional. Plates were set. Portions measured. Efficient.

The door opened.

His mother entered first, tired but alert in the way people became when they stepped out of work and back into something that mattered. His father followed shortly after, shoulders slightly hunched from the day.

They both paused when they saw him.

“You’re home early,” his mother said.

“Yes,” Nemo replied.

A brief silence.

“You had the ceremony today,” his father added.

“Yes.”

Another pause.

Nemo considered how to phrase it.

Then decided accuracy was sufficient.

“I awakened a class,” he said.

Both of them straightened.

“A class?” his mother repeated.

“Yes.”

“What kind?” his father asked, already leaning forward slightly.

“Assassin.”

The room went very still.

Then—

“That’s—” his father started.

“—a combat class,” his mother finished, eyes wide.

They looked at each other.

Then back at him.

And then, unexpectedly—

They smiled.

Not polite smiles. Not restrained ones.

Proud ones.

“That’s incredible,” his father said, voice warmer than Nemo had heard it in a while.

“You’ll go to college,” his mother added, already thinking ahead. “Proper training, better opportunities—”

“You won’t have to stay here,” his father said.

Nemo paused.

That had not been the intended outcome.

“I still intend to live quietly,” Nemo said.

“Yes, of course,” his mother replied immediately, still smiling. “Quietly. In a better place.”

“With options,” his father added.

“That’s what matters.”

Nemo looked between them.

They seemed… happy.

Genuinely.

He considered correcting them.

Explaining the risks. The expectations. The increased likelihood of complications.

He did not.

“…I see,” he said instead.

They sat down together to eat.

His parents talked more than usual. About possibilities. About the future. About things that, until now, had not been available to them.

Nemo listened.

Occasionally responding.

Quietly.

In the back of his mind, distant and persistent, something whispered again.

—*please*

Nemo focused on his meal confused, why do his parents truly think this is a good thing.
