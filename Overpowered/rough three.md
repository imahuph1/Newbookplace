# I Accidentally Awakened as a God and Still Failed Group Projects

## Chapter Three: A Reasonable Performance

The entrance examination was held the next day.

Nemo arrived early.

This was not by choice.

He had woken up early, left with what he believed was a reasonable amount of time, and then… arrived. The three-hour walk had taken something closer to twenty minutes.

He stood at the edge of the testing grounds, hands in his pockets, staring ahead.

“I will not think about that,” he decided.

Thinking about it would imply using it.

Using it would imply acknowledging it.

And acknowledging it would lead directly to problems.

So he ignored it.

---

The testing grounds were already active. Groups of students stood in loose clusters—stretching, comparing equipment, speaking with a level of confidence that suggested they had already decided the outcome.

Nemo avoided all of them.

All he needed to do was check in and disappear into the correct staging area. Minimal interaction. Maximum efficiency.

He approached the check-in desk.

“Name and school,” the clerk said without looking up.

“Nemo Nikt, PS 999.”

The clerk paused, then sighed.

“Quit joking around. I’m busy.”

“Nemo Nikt, PS 999.”

The clerk finally looked up.

There was a brief silence.

“You’re serious, this is not a setup, not some prank” they said.

“Nemo Nikt, PS 999” he handed over the paperwork.

The clerk flipped through a few pages, slower now.

“Huh. You do exist.” A pause. “I thought that was a joke name.”

“It is not.”

“Staging Area 10,” the clerk said, waving him off. “Good luck… Nobody.”

Nemo nodded once and left.

That had gone as expected.

---

He stood off to the side near his assigned area, observing in the least noticeable way possible.

There were, as expected, stronger candidates.

Well-equipped. Well-trained. Some already carried themselves like professionals. Their gear alone likely cost more than his entire building.

Good.

They would draw attention.

A horn sounded overhead.

A man stepped onto a raised platform, his voice carrying effortlessly.

“Welcome, applicants. Today you will be evaluated on three criteria: physical capability, combat aptitude, and control.”

Nemo listened.

“Your results will determine placement. High performers will be offered positions in elite academies. Others will be assigned accordingly.”

That sounded avoidable.

“There is no failure,” the man continued. “Only placement. Every combat class will attend a school. If you do not impress us today, you will still grow.”

Nemo nodded once.

The goal was simple.

Pass.

Not excel.

Remain precisely within the narrow band of acceptable competence.

Nothing more.

---

The first test was physical.

Nemo stepped forward when called.

“Name,” the examiner said.

“Nemo Nikt.”

The examiner glanced at the sheet, then chuckled before gesturing. “Strength first. Strike the target.”

A reinforced pillar stood before him, already marked with dents and cracks from previous attempts.

Nemo raised his hand.

Paused.

Adjusted.

Too little and he would fail.

Too much and people will notice.

He tapped the pillar.

A dull *thud*.

Nothing happened.

The examiner stared at it.

Then at Nemo.

“Again.”

Nemo nodded.

Slightly more force.

Carefully.

He struck the pillar a second time.

A small indentation formed—visible, but not impressive.

The examiner marked something down.

“Acceptable.”

Nemo stepped away.

Behind him, the next student stepped up and shattered part of the pillar outright.

Fragments cracked and split under the impact.

Nemo glanced back briefly.

“Useful,” he thought.

Whether that student was unusually strong or something else entirely, it did not matter.

They were now the focus.

---

Speed was next.

Nemo observed the runners before him—their pacing, their mistakes, their overconfidence.

When his turn came, he ran.

Not fast.

Not slow.

Precisely fast enough.

He finished comfortably in the middle.

No one paid attention.

---

Endurance followed.

A sustained run.

Nemo maintained a steady, controlled pace. Others pushed too hard and burned out. Some surged ahead, determined to stand out.

Nemo finished in the middle again.

The only unusual thing, Nemo was not breathing hard, no sweating, like he had just taken a mid-afternoon stroll not a ten-kilometer run.

One examiner frowned slightly.

Nemo did not react.

---

“Next: control.”

This was less structured.

Targets moved. Constructs shifted. Barriers reacted.

“Demonstrate your class ability,” an examiner instructed.

One by one, students displayed their skills—flashy, loud, visible.

Nemo waited.

Then his name was called.

“Assassin class,” the examiner noted. “Demonstrate.”

Nemo stepped forward.

Paused.

Assassin abilities were not meant to be seen.

That was the point.

He chose the simplest option.

He slipped into stealth. Not completely, just enough to show he was in stealth, a low level stealth.

To the other students, he blurred, noticeable if they were looking carefully, ignorable if they weren’t.

To the examiners, he remained visible.

Nemo moved.

Around the construct.

Behind it.

Backstab, a light strike, enough to destroy the target, precisely enough, no extra effort.

The target deactivated instantly.

He stepped back.

Minimal output. No excess.

“Control acceptable,” the examiner said, though more slowly this time.

At the edge of the field, one recruiter frowned slightly.

Something about the display felt deliberate.

As if the entire performance had been carefully acted.  Something about it seemed off, it was to perfectly average.

She made a note.

---

The final portion was combat aptitude.

Pairs were assigned.

Nemo checked the list.

His opponent ranked near the top.

“Inconvenient.”

The opponent approached confidently.

“You’re the Assassin?” they asked.

“Yes.”

“Try to keep up.”

Nemo said nothing.

The signal was given.

The opponent moved first—fast, aggressive, direct.

Nemo stepped aside.

Barely.

The strike missed.

Another came.

Then another.

Nemo avoided each one with minimal movement. No wasted motion. No visible strain.

To a casual observer, it looked like he was barely keeping up.

To anyone paying attention it was precise.

Very precise.

The opponent grew frustrated.

Then aggressive.

Then sloppy.

Nemo adjusted.

A single step forward.

A single motion.

He stopped.

The opponent froze.

A dull training dagger hovered a fraction of an inch from their throat.

No force.

No flourish.

Just inevitability.

The examiner raised a hand. “Enough.”

The match ended.

The opponent stepped back, frowning.

Nemo returned to his position, frowning perhaps he should have lost, taken longer, done something to show that he is not a combat master.

No applause.

No reaction.

Just notes.

That was ideal nobody seemed to notice the slip up.

---

At the end of the examination, results were posted.

Nemo reviewed his.

Mid-tier placement.

Above average.

Not exceptional.

He considered it.

“Acceptable, not perfect but close enough, still new to all of this, can't be perfectly average yet.”

---

As the crowd dispersed, an examiner reviewed her notes again.

She paused.

Then circled a line.

“Control: unusually high,” she muttered.

Another note followed.

“Likely suppressing output. Recruit.”

---

## Aurelis Arcane University

A knock echoed through the apartment the next morning.

Nemo did not move.

It was the weekend. His parents were home. This was, therefore, not his responsibility.

He rolled slightly onto his side and closed his eyes again.

The front door opened.

Voices.

Polite. Curious.

Then footsteps.

Approaching.

Closer.

The door to his small room opened.

Nemo did not move.

“Nemo,” his mother said gently. “There’s someone here to see you.”

He opened one eye.

“That seems unlikely.”

“They asked for you specifically.”

That was worse.

Nemo sat up slowly.

“I see.”

Nemo stepped out of his room a few minutes later.  A middle-aged woman sat at the table, paperwork laid out before her.  "Hello Nemo, I am Sarah Williams, I select candidates for Aurelis Arcane University, I am here today to invite you to join us."

Nemo looks at her, looks at the paperwork with a bit of fear and inevitability in his eyes "OK."

His mother steps in, "Please forgive him, he has always been like this, never good in conversation."

Sarah simply smiles, "I know all about him, I have access to his school jacket, I take no offense, it is just a bit of a shock to see it in person, they were not exaggeration at all where they."

While the women where talking and drinking tea Nemo was just looking at the paperwork with trepidation, the best he could think at least he does not have to fill out multiple applications.

Nemo sat their, signing what was placed in front of him while the adults chatted, deciding his future, nobody even consulted him, not that he minded, the less he has to do the better.  From what he gathered, the school is compulsory and government funded, minimum of two years, no maximum, although he will have to pay room and board after four years.  Level forty is the minimum graduation requirement.  Three dungeon runs a month is the minimum to show progress, classes are mandatory, there are secondary classes that are not, but the base classes are mandatory for the first year, they teach laws, responsibility, rules, and control.

Any resources that can be gathered from the dungeons can be sold to the school at just below market rates, the money can be spent on class specific resources or spent as the student likes.  The higher the level of the resources of course bring higher prices.  Rarity helps to of course.  One dungeon run with a team can bring in a month of his parents wages.  His parents look thrilled, before to long they may be able to move out of this apartment, to something a bit nicer.
