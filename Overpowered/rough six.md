# I Accidentally Awakened as a God and Still Failed Group Projects

## Chapter Six: Idiot Assassins

The alchemy lab was, for once, quiet.

Nemo approved.

A steady routine had formed over the past few weeks. Arrive. Work. Leave. Avoid unnecessary conversation. Avoid unnecessary attention.

It was efficient.

Across from him, Seraphine measured reagents with precise, practiced movements. She spoke when necessary. Not often.

This was also efficient.

Elias, unfortunately, was present at the table next to him.  Elias and his partner were the exact opposite of him and Seraphine,

“This reaction curve is wrong,” he muttered, adjusting notes. “It assumes stability at a threshold that clearly—”

“Yes,” Nemo said.

Elias paused.

“You’re not even looking.”

“I do not need to.”

“Right,” Elias said slowly. “That’s still concerning. How is it you are not the head of the class?"

Nemo only shrugged.

---

Nemo adjusted the heat on the burner.

Something felt wrong.

Not in the room, around it.

He did not react immediately.

He focused carefully reluctantly.

Information brushed against his awareness.

Movement, intent poorly concealed.

“No,” Nemo muttered.

Elias glanced up. “What?”

“Nothing.”

Which was not entirely accurate.

---

At the edge of the academy grounds, three figures moved.

They were dressed in dark clothing.

Which, in their minds, made them less noticeable.

It did not.

One of them tripped over a low stone.

Another attempted to help and walked directly into a decorative post.

The third stopped to whisper something urgent, and stepped into a shallow drainage channel.

There was a pause.

“We proceed,” one of them said, with the confidence of someone who should not have any.

---

Inside the lab, Nemo exhaled slowly.

*This is a problem,* he thought.

Not a large problem but an inconvenient one.

They were approaching the building.

Poorly.

Very poorly.

Nemo considered his options.

Ignoring it would result in interruption.

Interruption would result in attention.

Attention would result in questions.

Questions would interrupt routine, anything that interrupts routine is bad.

---

The first assassin reached the rear entrance.

He attempted to open the door.

It did not open.

He applied more force.

The handle came off in his hand.

He stared at it.

“This seems odd,” he whispered.

Behind him, the second assassin slipped on a wet stone from and fell directly into him.

The third attempted to catch them both and slipped on the same stone.

---

Inside the lab, Nemo adjusted the flame slightly.

Seraphine glanced at him.

"You seem distracted, everything ok?"

"Yes."

"Are you going to elaborate?"

"No."

"Of course not. Potion is approaching optimal temperature, time to add the next reagent."

---

The group made their way inside.

They moved through the hallway.

Quietly, in their opinion.

One of them knocked over a decorative stand.

They froze, “It was already unstable,” one whispered.

---

Nemo closed his eyes briefly.

*This is excessive,* he thought.

He shifted his focus.

A loose tile shifted under one assassin’s foot.

He stumbled and caught himself on a hanging banner which came loose and wrapped around him.

The other two stared.

---

Back in the lab, the mixture reached the correct temperature.

Nemo removed it from the flame.

“Done,” he said.

Seraphine nodded.

“Acceptable.”

---

The assassins reached the lab door.

One of them reached for the handle.

Paused.

“Ready?”

The other two nodded, they opened the door.

A cart rolled past in the hallway.

Unattended.

It clipped the door slamming it shut again.

The handle snapped, the trio retreated from the campus.  Today was not a good day.

---

That evening, Nemo lay on his bed.

Elias was reading quietly, for once.

Nemo closed his eyes.

He did not intend to engage.

He truly did not.

---

A faint sensation brushed against his awareness.

Familiar.

Unwanted.

Prayer.

Nemo exhaled slowly.

Multiple voices.

The same ones from earlier.

---

Against his better judgment—

He listened.

---

They knelt before a small shrine.

Bruised.

Disheveled.

Deeply confused.

“God of assassination,” the third finished.

There was a pause.

“We request guidance.”

---

The shrine flickered.

Darkness gathered, not dramatically, just enough.

A very disappointed god appeared before them.

---

“You are,” Nemo said, “the least competent assassins I have encountered.”

The three froze.

“We, thank you?” one tried.

“No,” Nemo said.

“That was not praise.”

---

They bowed lower.

“We sought to fulfill the contract—”

“You did not research the location,” Nemo said.

They froze.

“You did not assess the target.”

Silence.

“You did not investigate the contractor.”

A longer silence.

“You walked into an academy. In daylight. In uniform. Did you think you could access the target beneath the eyes of the dean, vice dean, instructors, all of higher level than you?”

One of them hesitated, opened his mouth, and thought better of it.

---

They remained bowed.

Very still.

“Why,” Nemo continued, “was this contract given to you?”

They did not answer.

“We were available?” one offered weakly.

Nemo closed his eyes.

“Do not contact me again.”

They flinched.

“Until you understand the answer to that question. Research the contractor, research the target, before you attempt to fulfill the contract a second time, know who, why, and where.  The world should not know the target is dead until you want them to know.”

Silence filled the space.

The presence faded.

The shrine dimmed.

The three remained where they were.

“We should research,” one said.

“Yes,” another agreed.

“Extensively,” the third added.

---

Back in his room, Nemo turned onto his side.

That had been—

Unnecessary.

But—

Preventing further problems—

Was acceptable.

“Idiots,” he muttered quietly.

Elias looked up from his book.

“Did you say something?”

“No.”

“Alright,” Elias said slowly.

---

Nemo closed his eyes.

The world, once again, was quiet.

## Interlude: Margins

Sarah Williams did not believe in coincidences.

Patterns, yes.

Negligence, often.

But not coincidences.

The report in front of her was, unfortunately, a pattern.

Nemo Nikt, family unremarkable, background unremarkable, entrance scores unremarkable.

That, on its own, would not have drawn attention.

Most students were unremarkable.

She turned the page.

Midterm evaluations.

Final assessments.

Practical scores.

All passing all unremarkable, but somehow his class standings were all at the fifty percent mark, perfectly average.

She paused.

Not approximately.

Not rounded.

Precisely.

Fifty.

Her pen tapped once against the desk.

“That’s not how averages work,” she said quietly.

She flipped to another section.

Different instructors.

Different grading styles.

Different rubrics.

One paper had a handwritten note from an instructor:

“Work meets requirements. No initiative shown.”

Another:

“Correct, but lacks depth.”

Another:

“Adequate.”

Sarah leaned back slightly.

Adequate, consistently, across unrelated disciplines, taught by unrelated instructors, evaluated under unrelated criteria was not a natural outcome.

It was a decision.

She turned the page.

Alchemy.

She paused again.

Ninety-eight.

Her pen stopped tapping.

She read the supporting notes.

Precise measurements. Efficient execution. Minimal waste. No errors of note.

A different instructor’s comment:

“Work exceeds expectations. Quiet but highly capable.”

Sarah set the report down.

Then picked it up again.

“Seraphine Vale,” she said, flipping to a secondary file.

Top-tier student. Consistently high performance. Strong theoretical and practical alignment.

She compared lab reports.

Side by side.

Seraphine’s work was excellent.

Nemo’s was cleaner.

Not better in outcome.

Better in process.

Fewer adjustments.

Fewer corrections.

No visible hesitation.

Sarah frowned slightly.

“Not that good, there is no way Seraphine elevated him that much if he is as average as he shows in other classes.” she murmured.

She turned the page.

Dungeon reports.

Participation logs.

Team feedback.

“Fulfills role.”
“Does assigned damage.”
“Does not communicate.”
“Would not select unless necessary.”

One report was more direct:

“Only take him if we need a DPS and no one else is available.”

Sarah’s pen resumed tapping.

Experience gain.

Level progression.

Average.

Exactly average.

She checked the numbers again.

Not slightly above.

Not slightly below.

Exactly.

Average.

She set the report down more carefully this time.

“That’s not how dungeons work,” she said.

Variance was expected.

Mistakes, inefficiencies, overperformance, underperformance—

That was normal.

Consistency like this was not.

She opened a third document.

Attendance.

Perfect.

Of course it was.

She closed it.

Then opened it again.

There were no disciplinary notes.

No commendations.

No incidents.

No anomalies.

Except the duel that did not happen.  The family that fell just before the duel was supposed to take place.

She looked back at the first page.

Fifty percent.

Alchemy: ninety-eight.

Dungeon progression: average.

She exhaled slowly.

“Either you are,” she said quietly,
“the most aggressively average student I have ever seen, or you are choosing to be.”

She flipped the file closed.

“Noted,” she said.

She did not file it away.

Instead, she set it aside.

"It appears I did not make a mistake in recruiting you, but how to force you to show how exceptional you are is still undecided."
