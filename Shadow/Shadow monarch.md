# 1 - Shadow Monarch
## I was supposed to die
It was just another night at the convenience store, the usual subject have come in and gone.  The drunks, the meth heads, the stoners, the other people who work odd shift hours.  Two AM, five hours left to go and I can go home and rest my eyes, play some games, do something, anything other than stand here listening to the hum of florescent bulbs and clean up after the ungrateful masses who seem to enjoy trashing the bathrooms.

The chime of the door gets my attention.  I put out the wet floor sign and head to the front.  I get two bullets in the chest for my effort.  Some young kid, probably not older than fifteen, took one hundred from the cash register and two cartons of Newport's.  He just left me there bleeding out on the floor.

The next thing I know I am waking up in the hospital.  The chemically cleaned air, and the same annoying buzz of florescent bulbs.  Next thing to hit me is the pain, a sharp pain in my chest, they apparently had to crack it open to remove one of the slugs.  Good luck getting any money out of me, not that I don't appreciate the skill of the surgeon and staff who saved my life, but I have no money, heck I make 7.25 an hour, just enough to pay for an apartment that makes solitary confinement look like a luxury hotel room.  No assets to speak of, no car, no phone, no TV, an old laptop that I pulled from the garbage and got running enough to play some older games. Heck I borrow the WIFI signal of the supermarket across the street.  

As i lay their wondering when the nurse is going to come in and check on me and the other fine gentlemen, wondering if I still have a job at the convenience store, that is when I am sure I have lost my mind.

There in a light translucent blue, in-front of my eyes was a screen, a character panel, for me.  No matter where I looked it was right there, I could see the world beyond it and my fingers could pass through it but it was there in my vision, like a web novel or isekai anime.  

"No," I whispered, my throat dry.  "No, that's not how this works."  Fantasy worlds are not real, Reincarnation is not real.  I am very much still in a hospital bed, connected by tubes and wires to monitors, full of pain and debt.
The text flickered, and vanished, almost like it was scared of me, or responding to my thoughts to go away.

The next few days passed in a blur.  Police came by, asked a few questions, and left never to touch this case again, but a police report is required for the boss.  Nurses and doctors came in at regular intervals, antibiotics and pain killers flowed into my veins.  I was allowed to stay five days until some bean counter upstairs figured out that I have no health insurance, and they where pretty sure I was not going to die from anything they did.  A bottle of antibiotics and a bill both me and the nurse knew was going to be sent to collections and written off followed me out the door in a set of paper cloths.  

After about a half hour on the bus I was back in my little apartment building.  A shower and change of cloths felt good.  I wandered down to the street and over the convenience store.  Boss man was there, as he was every day, sitting behind the counter chatting with the regular locals.  He looked up and there was a half smile when he saw me.  "So your alive." he said "Get some rest, same time tonight."
I smiled, at least I still had a job, I turned around and went back home, going to get a few hours of sleep before my shift begins.

# 2 - Night Shift Experiments

The store hadn't changed, it never changes.  The same flickering lights.  Same half-stocked shelves.  Same faint smell of burnt coffee and cleaning chemicals that never quite masked the rot underneath.  The blood stain had been cleaned up but was still faintly visible, the outline there if you knew where to look.   I knew where to look.

Two A.M. again, five hours left,  I stood behind the counter, letting the register hum do most of the work.  The wound on my chest still ached a little.  It was healing up nicely though, faster that I thought it should.  

The door chimed.  A drunk stumbled in, nodded at me like we were old friends, grabbed a six pack, and left exact change on the counter without a word.  Another regular followed, then another, then nothing.  The dead hour.  The stretch of time where you thoughts get loud enough to annoy you.

I cleaned, restocked, checked the cooler, counted cigarettes, did it all again to ensure everything was right. When I ran out of things to do I leaned against the counter and stared at the floor.

"What if it is real, how cool would that be."  I mean seriously how many people fantasize about super powers.  Everyone knows it is not real, can't really happen but it is still a fun thought experiment.  "I think I remember something about a storage space."  I felt ridiculous saying it out loud, still.  I focused on the empty coffee cup on the counter.  Nothing.

I looked at the cup, thinking to myself, "why am I surprised?"  Still, I was bored, and had nothing better to do, coffee was made and ready for the early risers.  I once again focused on the empty coffee cup and imagined it entering its own shadow.  Slowly the cup seamed to sink into its own shadow, suddenly it was gone.

"Well," I said aloud, more than shocked.  "That's new."

I waited for the panic to hit, the cup to reappear, the fantasy to end, it didn't.  Instead, a strange calm settled over me.  The same feeling I got when I figured out a tricky game mechanic after hours of trial and error.  Not excitement, understanding.  He somehow just knew how to put things in and take them out of storage, he knew what he had in storage.  As I scanned my storage space I found 72 shadow clones, waiting for command, and one coffee cup, empty.

My mind moved, willing the coffee cup to once again be on the counter, a shadow formed and the cup rose out of it sitting once again where it was.  I laughed, once, quiet and sharp, like I was afraid someone would hear, a bit of terror hidden within the laugh.  "Holy shit, it is real." 

The door chimed.  I jumped so hard I nearly knocked the cup over myself.  For once I was glad the security cameras where nothing more than show.  A guy in a yellow reflective construction jacket came in, grabbed an energy drink, paid without looking me in they eye, and left.

I experimented carefully after that.  A pen, a candy bar, a roll of quarters.  Everything went in and came back out exactly the same, no weight change, no damage, no delay.  

"What about… you know,"  I said, glancing around the empty store. "The system."  Nothing happened, no screen, no floating text.  No response whatsoever I exhaled, half relieved, half disappointed.
