# Chapter 1

## The Walk Back

Creating the Shadow Clone felt like someone had scooped the inside of John’s skull out with a spoon.

One moment he was sitting against the rock.

The next—

Darkness.

---

When John woke again, the world had changed colors.

Orange light painted the sky.

The sun was sinking behind the western hills, casting long shadows across the ravine.

“…Ugh.”

His entire body felt like it had been wrung out like an old cloth.

Dry.

Empty.

He pushed himself upright slowly.

For a moment he thought the fall had finally finished him off.

Then a faint blue window appeared in front of his eyes.

---

**Mana: 100%**  

**Status: Recovered**  

---

John groaned.

“Right… the clone.”

The memory came back quickly.

Shadow Echo.

Creating the clone had drained him completely.

Whatever magic powered this system clearly didn’t come for free.

He rubbed his temples.

“At least I didn’t die.”

His injuries still hurt, but they weren’t as bad as he expected. The body had taken a brutal fall, but apparently not a fatal one.

Adrenaline and whatever strange influence the system had on him were keeping him functional.

John looked up toward the cliff again.

It was still steep.

Still ugly.

Still very climbable.

“Guess I’m walking home.”

Another small memory from the body surfaced.

The village wasn’t far.

Maybe a couple miles through the surrounding woods.

Normally it wouldn’t be a dangerous journey.

But dusk was falling.

And that meant the local predators would be coming out.

Thankfully, the area near the village wasn’t known for particularly dangerous monsters.

Low-level beasts.

Wolves.

Boars.

Maybe a few oversized predators.

Nothing too terrifying.

John closed his eyes and focused inward.

“Shadow Space.”

The world shifted.

For a brief moment he felt his mind brush against a vast black expanse.

Inside it stood his Shadow Clone.

The copy was already moving.

Practicing footwork.

Shadowboxing with invisible opponents.

John blinked.

“Well… that’s efficient.”

His attention shifted to the other objects stored there.

Weapons.

Waiting.

He focused on one.

“Summon.”

Something cold and solid appeared in his right hand.

Steel flashed in the fading sunlight.

A slender blade.

Elegant.

Deadly.

A rapier.

John turned it slightly, admiring the balance.

Despite never holding one before, his body instinctively understood how to grip it.

*Rapier – Damage: 2 × Strength*  

“Not bad.”

He pushed himself fully to his feet and began climbing the slope out of the ravine.

Loose dirt shifted under his boots but the climb was manageable.

Within ten minutes he reached the top.

The forest stretched ahead.

Tall trees.

Darkening shadows.

And the narrow dirt road that led back to the village.

John stepped onto the path and began walking.

The rapier rested comfortably in his hand.

His senses felt… sharper.

The system might not have shown his stats yet, but the Shadow Clone boost meant he was already stronger than this body should have been.

Stronger than most villagers.

Stronger than the boys who pushed him.

The thought made his lips curl slightly.

But revenge could wait.

First he needed to survive the night.

The forest grew darker as he walked.

Insects hummed.

Leaves rustled in the wind.

Then—

A low growl.

John stopped.

From the bushes beside the road something moved.

Two yellow eyes appeared in the shadows.

A lean gray shape stepped out.

Wolf.

Thin.

Hungry.

Probably drawn by the scent of blood.

John sighed quietly.

“Of course.”

The wolf circled slowly.

Testing.

Predators near villages were rarely strong.

Most were barely stronger than animals.

Level one or two at most.

Still dangerous for an unarmed farmer.

But John wasn’t unarmed.

He adjusted his grip on the rapier.

The wolf lunged.

Fast.

Teeth flashing.

John stepped sideways.

His body moved almost instinctively.

The rapier shot forward.

Steel pierced fur.

The blade slid cleanly between the animal’s ribs.

The wolf yelped once before collapsing.

John pulled the blade free.

Blood dripped onto the dirt.

He stared down at the corpse for a moment.

“…That was easier than expected.”

A faint blue message appeared.

---

**Enemy Defeated**  

**Experience Gained**  

---

John smiled slightly.

“So it works.”

He wiped the blade on the grass and continued walking.

The forest grew darker.

Night creatures were beginning to stir.

A rustle ahead.

Then another.

Two more shapes moved through the trees.

Smaller predators this time.

Fox-sized creatures with sharp teeth and glowing eyes.

John rolled his shoulders.

“Well.”

He raised the rapier.

“Looks like dinner’s coming to me.”

The creatures lunged from opposite sides.

Steel flashed again.

The walk home had just begun.

## The remains

By the time John reached the outskirts of the village, night had nearly fallen.

The sky was deep purple now, the last light of the sun fading behind the distant hills. The dirt road curved through a stretch of farmland and small wooden homes.

Or what used to be homes.

John slowed as he walked.

Something was wrong.

Too quiet.

No lanterns.

No voices.

No smell of cooking fires.

Instead there was another smell.

Smoke.

Old smoke.

His chest tightened.

The body’s memories stirred again, pulling him forward.

He rounded the final bend in the road.

And stopped.

The small house at the edge of the village was gone.

Not abandoned.

Not damaged.

Gone.

Blackened wooden beams stuck out of a pile of ash and collapsed timber. The roof had caved inward. Charred boards lay scattered across the dirt.

The entire house had burned.

John didn’t move for a long time.

Wind whispered through the ruins.

Ash drifted lazily across the ground.

Slowly, mechanically, he stepped forward.

The door no longer existed.

Just a black opening where the entrance had been.

He stepped inside.

The smell hit him immediately.

Burnt wood.

Burnt cloth.

And something worse.

The bedroom was still partially intact. One wall had collapsed inward, leaving the old bed visible beneath broken beams.

Someone lay there.

John already knew.

He walked forward anyway.

The woman on the bed looked small.

Too small.

Her hair had burned away in patches, and the blankets were blackened from smoke, but the fire hadn't fully consumed her.

She had died before it spread.

Her hands were still folded across her stomach.

John stared down at the corpse.

His mind filled with memories again.

A tired woman working late by candlelight.

Calloused hands sewing torn clothes.

A soft voice telling the boy not to worry.

*“Things will get better.”*  

The body’s mother.

She had worked herself to exhaustion just to keep them alive.

And now she was dead.

John didn’t need the system to tell him who had done it.

Four boys.

Arrogant.

Cruel.

The sons of powerful families.

They had pushed the boy off a cliff.

Then they burned his home.

Just to erase the problem.

John felt something inside his chest go very still.

Not rage.

Not yet.

Just a cold understanding.

He wasn’t strong enough.

Not yet.

If he marched into town now, they would crush him.

Their fathers had guards.

Money.

Influence.

Power.

John stood there for several minutes in silence.

Finally he stepped forward and gently pulled the blanket up over the woman’s face.

“I’m… sorry,” he said quietly.

The words felt strange coming from his mouth.

They weren’t his mother.

But the boy whose body he now wore had loved her.

And now both of them were gone.

John turned and walked out of the burned house.

The night air felt cold.

Above him the stars had begun to appear.

The village lay silent.

Nobody came.

Nobody asked questions.

Nobody helped.

Of course they didn’t.

People with powerful parents didn’t get blamed for things like this.

John already knew that from his first life.

He looked toward the center of town.

Somewhere there were four boys laughing about what they had done.

Drinking.

Boasting.

Certain there would never be consequences.

John slowly turned away.

“Not yet,” he said to himself.

Revenge taken too early was just suicide.

And he had no intention of dying again.

Instead he looked toward the dark line of forest beyond the village.

Deep inside those woods was something every frontier settlement had.

A dungeon.

The kind that adventurers used to grow stronger.

Dangerous.

But full of monsters.

And monsters meant experience.

Levels.

Power.

John tightened his grip on the rapier.

The blade reflected the starlight.

“If I want revenge…”

His voice was calm.

“I’ll earn it.”

He stepped off the road and walked toward the forest.

Behind him the ruins of the burned house faded into darkness.

Ahead of him waited monsters.

And the dungeon.

John didn’t look back.
