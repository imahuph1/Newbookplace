# Fates Disturbance

## Prolog

Mike had an average day.  He spent most of it repairing machines that should have been replaced twenty years ago. Somehow, it was still cheaper to keep patching them together than to buy new ones. Not that he complained much. The pay was steady, the work wasn't especially difficult, and after three decades in maintenance, he knew the equipment better than the people who owned it.

That evening was just as ordinary.  The news was full of the usual nonsense. Corrupt politicians accusing corrupt CEOs of greed and misconduct. The CEOs firing back with accusations of their own. Everyone shouting. Everyone pretending to care.

Nothing would come of it.  The arguments were little more than a stage play, performed for the public's benefit. A distraction to keep people angry, entertained, and divided.

Even knowing that, it still irritated him.  The corruption ran so deep that sometimes it felt as though the entire country needed to be torn down and rebuilt from the ground up. Government, corporations, institutions—every rotten piece of it.

Humanity had always been good at finding ways to abuse power.

Mike, however, was just another cog in the machine.  If he quit tomorrow, nobody would notice. If he stood up and fought back, the machine would grind him into dust without slowing for a second.

A couple of hours and a few drinks later, he finally turned off the news. There was only so much outrage a man could stomach in one evening.

He switched over to some mindless sitcom and let the canned laughter fill the room.  As the show played in the background, his thoughts drifted.

He imagined what it would be like to have real power. The power to hold people accountable. The power to force justice onto those who spent fortunes avoiding it. Men and women who could ruin thousands of lives and walk away with nothing worse than a fine they could pay from a single day's earnings.

It was a childish fantasy.  He knew that.  Still, it was a pleasant one.

Leaning back in his recliner, Mike drained the last of his drink and set the empty bottle on the side table.

Tomorrow was his fiftieth birthday.  He had the day off.  For once, there would be no alarms, no broken machines, no supervisors demanding miracles from worn-out equipment.  Just a quiet morning and the chance to sleep in.

With that comforting thought, his eyelids grew heavy.  Within minutes, he was asleep.

## Chapter One

Sunlight filtering through a canopy of leaves slowly pulled Mike from unconsciousness.  His first thought was that this was an incredibly realistic dream.  Then the pain hit.

Every muscle in his body ached. His ribs felt as though they had been crushed in a vise, and each breath sent a fresh wave of agony through his chest. A groan escaped his lips before he could stop it.

What the hell happened?

For a brief moment, he wondered if he'd somehow gone out after drinking and gotten himself into a bar fight. It was a ridiculous thought. He hadn't been in a fight since high school, and at fifty years old he certainly wasn't about to start now.

Closing his eyes, he focused on breathing through the pain.  As the initial shock faded, he tried to remember the previous night.

He had come home from work, watched the news, had a few drinks, watched television, and gone to sleep.

That was it, no bars, no parties, No drunken adventures.  Just an ordinary evening.

Mike opened his eyes again and immediately realized something was very wrong. He wasn't in his recliner.  He wasn't in his house.  Hell, he wasn't even indoors.

He was lying in a patch of grass beneath towering trees. Shafts of sunlight broke through the leaves overhead, painting the forest floor in shifting patterns of gold and green.

The air smelled of damp earth and growing things.  Birds chirped somewhere in the distance.  An insect buzzed past his ear.  There were no sirens, no traffic, none of the noises that he heard every day of his life.  Nothing but his own ragged breathing and the sounds of nature.

A chill ran down his spine.  This wasn't a dream.

And if it wasn't a dream...Where am I?

Mike pushed himself upright and immediately regretted it. Pain exploded through his body, forcing him to grit his teeth to keep from crying out. His arms trembled under the effort, far weaker than they should have been. Confused, he held one hand in front of his face.

The hand staring back at him was not his.  It was small, thin, covered in scratches and bruises. The calluses he had earned from decades of working with tools were gone, replaced by the smooth skin of a child.  His breath caught in his throat.

Panic surged through him as he looked down at himself. The clothes hanging from his body were little more than torn rags, stained with dirt and dried blood. Skinny arms. Skinny legs. A body that couldn't have belonged to anyone older than six or seven.

Screaming he stumbled back, the voice that came out of his throat was not his.  Small, high pitched, not the voice of a fifty year old man, but that of a pre-pubescent child.

As his back hit a tree the pain brought darkness to his eyes.  Mike welcomed the void with open arms as he passed out.

Some time later he awoke, his mouth felt like it was full of cotton. "I need water." Mike thought to himself.  As he opened his eyes and looked around the reality of his situation hit him.
