# Fates Disturbance

Mike had an average day.  He spent most of it repairing machines that should have been replaced twenty years ago. Somehow, it was still cheaper to keep patching them together than to buy new ones. Not that he complained much. The pay was steady, the work wasn't especially difficult, and after three decades in maintenance, he knew the equipment better than the people who owned it.

That evening was just as ordinary.  The news was full of the usual nonsense. Corrupt politicians accusing corrupt CEOs of greed and misconduct. The CEOs firing back with accusations of their own. Everyone shouting. Everyone pretending to care.

Nothing would come of it.  The arguments were little more than a stage play, performed for the public's benefit. A distraction to keep people angry, entertained, and divided.

Even knowing that, it still irritated him.  The corruption ran so deep that sometimes it felt as though the entire country needed to be torn down and rebuilt from the ground up. Government, corporations, institutions—every rotten piece of it.

Humanity had always been good at finding ways to abuse power.

Mike, however, was just another cog in the machine.  If he quit tomorrow, nobody would notice. If he stood up and fought back, the machine would grind him into dust without slowing for a second.

A couple of hours and a few drinks later, he finally turned off the news. There was only so much outrage a man could stomach in one evening.

He switched over to some mindless sitcom and let the canned laughter fill the room.  As the show played in the background, his thoughts drifted.

He imagined what it would be like to have real power. The power to hold people accountable. The power to force justice onto those who spent fortunes avoiding it. Men and women who could ruin thousands of lives and walk away with nothing worse than a fine they could pay from a single day's earnings.

It was a childish fantasy.  He knew that.  Still, it was a pleasant one.

Leaning back in his recliner, Mike drained the last of his drink and set the empty bottle on the side table.

Tomorrow was his fiftieth birthday.  He had the day off.  For once, there would be no alarms, no broken machines, no supervisors demanding miracles from worn-out equipment.  Just a quiet morning and the chance to sleep in.

With that comforting thought, his eyelids grew heavy.  Within minutes, he was asleep.
