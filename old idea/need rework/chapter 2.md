The panic faded.

Jimmy lay still for a moment, breathing slowly, letting the unfamiliar comfort of the bed sink in. The ceiling above him was smooth plaster, not cracked stone or rotting wood. The sheets were warm. Clean. Nothing smelled of mold or refuse.

_I’m safe,_ he told himself.

For now.

He sat up and swung his legs over the side of the bed. His body moved easily—too easily. There was a lightness to him that hadn’t been there yesterday, a quiet strength coiled beneath his skin. When he stood, there was no stiffness, no ache from sleeping on hard ground. His balance was perfect, effortless.

Jimmy flexed his fingers.

_Body Refinement, twenty percent…_

He exhaled slowly. This wasn’t imagination. This was real.

He washed his face, dressed, and hesitated only a moment before withdrawing the sword from his system storage.

The blade appeared soundlessly in his hand.

It was simple in design—no excessive ornamentation, no jewels or inscriptions shouting importance. The steel was pale and clean, the edge impossibly sharp. When Jimmy held it, it felt… right. Balanced. As though it had been waiting for him.

> **Tier Three Sword**\
> **Status:** Bound\
> **Compatibility:** Optimal

He performed a single, cautious swing.

The air parted.

Jimmy froze, heart hammering. The movement had been controlled, restrained—yet it felt as though he’d barely restrained something far more dangerous. He sheathed the blade at once and returned it to storage.

_Not here,_ he decided. _Not indoors._

The city was already stirring as dawn crept across the streets. Vendors prepared stalls, guards rotated shifts, and awakened youths—some still flushed with excitement from the day before—gathered in small groups.

Jimmy moved with purpose.

Every city had one.

The Dungeon Hall sat near the eastern wall, a squat stone building reinforced with array-marked pillars. Above its entrance hung a simple iron plaque:

## First Steps Outside the Walls

Morning light filtered through the city as Jimmy stepped out of The Cloud Hotel.

The streets were already awake. Vendors shouted prices, carts rattled over stone, and the smell of fresh bread mingled with less pleasant odors drifting up from the gutters. People moved with purpose—workers heading to jobs, merchants opening stalls, newly awakened youths walking with a little extra pride in their steps.

Jimmy blended in.

That alone was new.

Clean clothes. Clean skin. A straight-backed posture that came naturally now instead of being forced. No one looked at him twice. No one watched him like he might steal something if they turned away.

He adjusted the strap of his pack and headed toward the eastern gate.

The gate district sat at the edge of the city’s safe zone, where the protection of the Ascension Temple thinned but didn’t vanish entirely. Beyond it lay farmland, ruins, and the lowest-tier dungeons—places where beginners tested their strength and veterans harvested easy resources.

A stone obelisk marked the dungeon entrance area. Faded runes pulsed faintly along its surface.

**Lair of the great boar**\
**Recommended Level: 1–10.**\


Jimmy stopped a short distance away and took a slow breath.

This was the line.

Once he crossed it, there is no going back.

He hesitated only briefly.

“Easy first,” he muttered.

The Easy-mode dungeon felt… polite.

The tunnels were wider, the creatures slower. Attacks telegraphed themselves half a second before landing, as if the dungeon itself wanted to be sure he was paying attention.

Jimmy was.

The sword moved when he wanted it to, where he wanted it to. Beasts fell in single cuts, their attacks never coming close enough to matter. He didn’t even need to think about positioning.

The boss—a smaller version of the Alpha—went down just as cleanly.

> **Dungeon Cleared.**\
> **Clear Rating:** S\
> **Drops Increased Slightly.**

Jimmy nodded.

Easy lived up to its name.

***

### Normal Mode

Normal was familiar.

This was what he’d already experienced—beasts that could hurt someone careless, but nothing that demanded urgency from him. Jimmy flowed through the dungeon, adjusting his steps without conscious thought, blade flashing in efficient arcs.

He took a glancing hit once, claws scraping harmlessly across his armor.

> **Armor Integrity Stable.**

He barely felt it.

The Normal-mode Alpha lasted a heartbeat longer than the first.

> **Dungeon Cleared.**\
> **Clear Rating:** S\
> **Body Refinement increased: 47% → 48%**

Jimmy exhaled, more out of habit than need.

“So far so good.”

He looked at the final option.

> **Hard Mode Available**

Jimmy frowned.

Hard meant better drops. Hard meant more gold.

Hard also meant risk.

He glanced up at the sky. The sun was still high. Plenty of time. And this was still a low-tier dungeon, rated for beginners.

“I won’t be stupid,” he told himself.

He selected _Enter_.

***

### Hard Mode

The moment the dungeon formed around him, Jimmy felt it.

The air was heavier. The tunnels narrower. The ambient mana pressed against his skin instead of drifting lazily through it.

The first beast came fast.

Too fast.

Jimmy cut it down—but its claws grazed his side as it fell. The impact jolted him, knocking the breath from his lungs.

He retreated two steps, heart racing.

_That was closer than it should have been._

The next fight forced him to move. Not just step, but _dodge_. A lunging beast slammed into the wall where his head had been a moment earlier, stone cracking under the impact.

Jimmy’s sword took it apart—but his pulse didn’t slow.

By the time he reached the core chamber, his breathing had changed.

The Hard-mode Alpha was larger. Heavier. Its movements were abrupt and violent, charging without warning, smashing stone with sheer mass.

Jimmy went to meet it head-on.

That was his mistake.

The Alpha twisted mid-charge, shoulder slamming into him like a battering ram. Jimmy flew, crashing into the wall hard enough to rattle his teeth.

Pain flared.

Real pain.

> **Warning: Minor Internal Injury Detected.**

Jimmy rolled aside just as tusks tore through the space where his torso had been.

He didn’t stand immediately.

Instead, he pushed off the ground and _moved_—sideways, backward, around. The sword followed, cutting when openings appeared instead of forcing them.

The fight stretched longer than any before it.

When the Alpha finally fell, Jimmy was breathing hard, blood trickling from the corner of his mouth.

The dungeon cleared.

> **Dungeon Cleared.**\
> **Clear Rating:** A\
> **Bonus Drops Reduced Due to Injury.**\
> **Body Refinement increased: 48% → 49%**

Jimmy leaned against the wall until the exit portal formed.

***

### After

Back in the city, Jimmy sat heavily on a bench near the gate, staring at nothing.

He wasn’t angry.

He was thoughtful.

“I hit hard,” he murmured. “Harder than most.”

That wasn’t the problem.

The problem was that he’d let the fight happen on the enemy’s terms.

No footwork. No burst movement. No way to disengage when strength alone wasn’t enough.

Jimmy opened the system store.

> **Tier One Store – Available Categories:**\
> • Cultivation Manuals\
> • Techniques\
> • Body Refinement\
> • Movement Arts

He stared at the last option for a long moment.

“…Mobility,” he said finally.

Because next time, he wasn’t planning on getting hit at all.

## Watched

Jimmy felt it halfway back to the city.

Not danger.

Attention.

It wasn’t the sharp prickle he associated with ambushes or the creeping dread of being out too close to nightfall. This was subtler—like a weight on his back that shifted when he moved.

He didn’t react.

That was habit. On the streets, you learned quickly that looking first often made things worse.

Jimmy kept his pace steady as he crossed fully back into the temple’s safe zone. Only when the stone beneath his feet changed from dirt-packed road to worn city paving did he let his shoulders relax a fraction.

The feeling didn’t go away.

_So they’re patient,_ he thought.

He turned down a side street instead of heading straight toward the inn district. Not an alley—those attracted the wrong kind of attention—but a narrow trade lane lined with closed stalls and stacked crates. Busy enough to be normal. Quiet enough to notice things.

Jimmy stopped at a fruit stand and picked up an apple.

“Two copper,” the vendor said.

Jimmy paid through the system. The transaction completed with a faint chime.

As he bit into the apple, he shifted his stance slightly and let his gaze wander.

There.

Across the street, reflected faintly in a shop window, a man stood near a lamppost. Middle-aged. Plain clothes. No visible weapon. His posture was relaxed in the way of someone who didn’t expect trouble.

When Jimmy looked directly at him, the man didn’t flinch.

He smiled.

That was new.

Jimmy finished the apple, tossed the core into a waste bin, and continued walking. He didn’t head home. He didn’t change pace. He simply… wandered.

The man followed.

Not closely. Not obviously. He kept to the far side of the street, stopping when Jimmy stopped, pretending to browse when Jimmy slowed. Professional, but not aggressive.

_Guild,_ Jimmy decided. Or someone working for one.

They didn’t recruit loudly. They didn’t force offers. They watched.

Jimmy turned another corner and nearly ran into a patrol.

City guards in temple-marked armor stood at an intersection, chatting idly. Their presence thinned the feeling of pressure immediately.

When Jimmy glanced back, the man was gone.

He didn’t relax.

He waited until he reached The Cloud Hotel, then climbed the stairs to his room and locked the door behind him.

Only then did he sit on the bed and exhale.

“So,” he said softly. “I’ve been noticed.”

That had been inevitable. Clearing a dungeon on Hard mode, even a low-tier one, didn’t go completely unseen. Drops had to be sold. Records had to be logged. The system itself remembered everything.

He pulled up his storage and examined the items again—cores, hides, the Alpha Fang. Nothing flashy. Nothing that screamed wealth.

Good.

Jimmy lay back on the bed, staring at the ceiling.

_They didn’t approach,_ he thought. _They didn’t threaten._

Which meant they wanted something.

Talent. Potential. Control.

Or simply to know whether he was worth paying attention to.

The system chimed softly.

> **Reminder:** Nightfall in three hours.

Jimmy smiled faintly.

“Plenty of time,” he murmured.

Outside, somewhere in the city, a man in plain clothes filed away a quiet report—nothing urgent, nothing alarming.

Just a note.

_Sixteen. Newly awakened. Cleared low-tier dungeon Hard mode solo._
_Highly observant, noticed my observations_
_Recommend continued observation._
