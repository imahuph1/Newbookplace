Mike had been training hard for as long as he could remember.

Seventeen years old, and he was still barely clinging to the **first rank of Martial Apprentice**.

His body was strong enough. His stance was correct. He understood the basics of sword and fist well enough to teach beginners. But no matter how many times he practiced, no matter how carefully he followed the manuals, his power refused to gather properly. His strikes dispersed instead of condensing. His energy slipped through him like water through open fingers.

Talentless.

That was the word no one said out loud anymore.

If he failed to reach the **second stage of Martial Apprentice** before the year ended, his fate would be sealed. He would be registered as a civilian—no cultivation rights, no access to manuals, no protection under martial law. Just another body to be fed into factories, docks, or construction crews. Long hours. Minimal pay. A life where even speaking back to a martial practitioner could cost him everything.

In this world, strength wasn’t just power.

It was permission to exist.

The sun had already set by the time Mike left the training hall. His arms trembled from overuse, his breathing uneven. Today had been no different from the hundreds before it—forms repeated until his muscles screamed, controlled breathing until his lungs burned, focused circulation that went nowhere.

No breakthrough.\
Not even a hint.

The communal showers were empty, steam curling lazily toward the ceiling. Mike stepped under the spray and let cool water pour over his head, washing away sweat, dust, and frustration. He rested his hands against the tiled wall, forehead pressed forward, eyes closed.

_What am I doing wrong?_

He had copied the instructors perfectly. He had trained longer than most. Harder than many. And yet—

A sharp, unfamiliar sound cut through the steady rush of water.

_Ding._

Mike stiffened.

The noise was crisp and mechanical, completely out of place in the quiet shower room.

Before he could react, translucent blue text flickered into existence in front of his eyes.

**\[Initializing System…]**

His breath caught.

The water kept running. The world didn’t pause. But Mike’s heart began to pound as if it might burst from his chest.

**\[Error detected: Excessive effort usage.]**\
**\[Recalculating growth parameters…]**

Mike blinked hard. The text didn’t disappear.

“…What?” he whispered.

**\[Lazy System successfully bound.]**

The words lingered, steady and undeniable, as if they had always been meant for him.

Somewhere deep in his chest, something loosened—just a little.

The blue screen rippled once, then stabilized in front of Mike’s eyes.

***

**\[Lazy System – Tutorial Mode Initiated]**

**\[Host Detected: Mike Carter]**\
**\[Age: 17]**\
**\[Cultivation Rank: Martial Apprentice – Stage 1 (Unstable)]**\
**\[Evaluation: Excessive Effort / Critically Inefficient]**

Mike swallowed. The text was painfully accurate.

“What… is this?” he muttered.

The system did not answer the question. Instead, new lines appeared, each word precise and merciless.

***

### **\[System Principle]**

> **Effort beyond necessity disrupts natural optimization.**\
> **Forced growth results in energy leakage and stagnation.**\
> **True improvement occurs during non-action.**

Mike frowned. _Non-action?_

***

### **\[Primary Rule – Lazy Growth]**

> **The less effort the host exerts toward self-improvement, the greater the system’s compensation.**

**Effort Level → Growth Conversion**

* **Complete idleness** → ×100 growth efficiency

* **Casual activity (resting, leisure, observation)** → ×20

* **Normal daily movement** → ×5

* **Intentional training** → ×0.1

* **Forced training / combat under pressure** → ×0.01

* **Obsessive effort detected** → **Growth suppression**

Mike stared.

“So… training makes me weaker?”

***

### **\[Secondary Rule – Passive Mastery]**

> **Understanding requires observation, not repetition.**

* Martial techniques can be comprehended through **passive exposure**

* Flaws, inefficiencies, and redundancies are **automatically highlighted**

* Optimized versions are **integrated without practice**

**Note:**

> _Active rehearsal counts as effort and will reduce gains._

His mouth went dry.

All those years of drilling. All that pain.

Wrong.

***

### **\[Tertiary Rule – Flow State]**

> **The system operates only when the host acts without intent to improve.**

* Thinking _“I want to get stronger”_ reduces effectiveness

* Acting naturally restores full function

* Letting go maximizes growth

Mike exhaled slowly. That explained it. Every time he pushed harder, the power slipped away. Every time he obsessed over breakthroughs, his body rebelled.

***

### **\[Warning]**

> **The Lazy System is exclusive.**\
> **Imitation without compatibility will result in zero gains or backlash.**

Mike almost laughed.

They’d never believe him anyway.

***

### **\[Initial Compensation Granted]**

**\[Detected: 7 years of excessive effort and suppressed growth]**\
**\[Converting stored inefficiency…]**

The air around him seemed to thicken.

**\[Martial Apprentice – Stage 1 → Stage 2]**\
**\[Stability: Perfect]**

Mike’s knees buckled. He caught himself against the wall as something _settled_ inside him—his energy no longer scattered, no longer slipping away. It gathered smoothly, naturally, like it had always known where to go.

He hadn’t trained.

He hadn’t focused.

He had done nothing.

***

The system delivered its final line.

**\[Suggestion:]**

> _Sit down. Rest. Do not try._

The blue text faded.

The water kept running.

Mike let out a shaky breath, sank down onto the cool tile, and for the first time in his life…

Stopped trying.

Mike slept.

Not the half-sleep he’d known for years—no anxious drifting, no replaying forms in his head, no calculating how many hours he could squeeze in before dawn. This sleep was deep and heavy, like his body had been waiting for permission all along.

When he opened his eyes, sunlight was already creeping through the thin curtains.

He didn’t panic.

That alone felt wrong.

For years, waking late meant failure. Missed training. Lost ground. Today, he just stared at the ceiling and breathed.

Slow. Easy.

**\[Lazy System Notification:]**\
**Effort Level: None Detected**\
**Growth Efficiency: ×100 (Sustained)**

Mike closed his eyes again.

“…Figures.”

Eventually, biology won out. He dragged himself out of bed, shuffled to the bathroom, and took care of his daily constitutional with the same unhurried apathy. No circulating energy. No posture corrections. No breath control.

Just existing.

**\[Passive Stabilization Ongoing.]**

When he came back out, he paused in the middle of the room.

The training sword leaned against the wall where it always had. Polished grip. Worn guard. Countless hours burned into its shape.

Mike looked at it for a long moment.

Then he turned away.

He flopped back onto the bed, grabbed the remote, and turned on the TV. Some morning program droned on about upcoming tournaments and rising young geniuses. People his age. Younger, even.

He muted it.

The ceiling fan hummed. The room was warm. Comfortable.

“To hell with it all,” he muttered, tossing the remote aside.\
“I’m done trying.”

He didn’t circulate energy.\
Didn’t check his rank.\
Didn’t even _think_ about getting stronger.

He just lay there, watching dust drift through sunlight, letting his thoughts dissolve into nothing.

***

**\[Lazy System Notification:]**\
**Host has entered True Non-Action State.]**\
**Converting rest into foundational reinforcement…**\
**Muscle memory refined. Energy pathways smoothed. Waste eliminated.]**

Something shifted inside him.

Not explosively.\
Not dramatically.

It felt like finally sitting down after standing for too long.

***

Hours later, someone knocked on his door.

Mike groaned and rolled onto his side, pulling the blanket over his head.

“…Go away.”

**\[Growth Efficiency: ×120 — Mental Resistance Dropped.]**

The knock came again, sharper this time.

Mike sighed, sat up, and rubbed his face.

“…Fine.”

As he stood, a strange thought drifted through his mind—idle, unimportant, barely formed.

_Huh. I feel lighter._

The system did not comment.

It didn’t need to.

The knock came again—louder this time.

Mike groaned, dragging the blanket off his head. He glanced at the clock. Late morning.

He hadn’t missed sunrise training in **years**.

“…I’m coming,” he muttered.

He shuffled to the door and pulled it open.

A familiar face stood in the hallway.

“Mike?”

It was **Evan**—his training partner. Same age, broader shoulders, permanently tired eyes. His hair was still damp from morning drills, training uniform wrinkled and stained with sweat. He looked Mike up and down like he was seeing a ghost.

“You good, man?” Evan asked. “You didn’t show up.”

Mike leaned against the doorframe, scratching his head. “Yeah. I’m fine.”

Evan frowned. “You never miss sunrise. Not once. Thought you passed out in the hall or something.”

Mike shrugged.

The motion felt… easy.

**\[Lazy System: Growth Efficiency Sustained.]**

Evan noticed it too. His eyes narrowed slightly. Martial practitioners learned to sense changes in posture, weight, breathing.

“You feel different,” Evan said slowly. “Did you… break through?”

Mike almost laughed.

“Not really.”

Evan snorted. “Figures. If anyone deserves it—”

He stopped mid-sentence.

“…Wait.”

Evan took a step closer, brow furrowing. He wasn’t circulating energy, but instinct kicked in anyway. His gaze sharpened, like he was trying to line up something that refused to stay still.

Mike felt it then—the subtle pressure of someone _looking_.

Evan swallowed. “You’re not leaking.”

Mike blinked. “What?”

“Your energy,” Evan said. “It’s… quiet. Like it’s sitting where it’s supposed to.”

That shouldn’t have been possible. Not for someone stuck at first rank.

Mike shifted his weight, uncomfortable. “I just took the day off.”

Evan stared at him. “You don’t take days off.”

“Yeah,” Mike said, glancing back at his bed, the TV still murmuring softly. “I guess I do now.”

There was a long silence.

Evan finally shook his head. “Look, I don’t care what you’re doing. Just—come by the gym later, alright? Instructor Liang’s already in a bad mood. He noticed.”

Mike winced. That wasn’t good.

“I’ll think about it.”

Evan’s expression tightened. “You always think about it. You always show up.”

Mike met his eyes.

“Not today.”

Evan hesitated, then nodded slowly. “Okay. Just… don’t disappear on us.”

He turned and walked down the hall, then stopped.

“Mike?”

“Yeah?”

Evan didn’t turn around. “If you figured something out…”

He paused, then exhaled.

“…don’t burn yourself out again.”

The door closed softly.

Mike leaned back against it, heart steady, breathing calm.

**\[Lazy System Notification:]**\
**Social interaction completed without exertion.]**\
**Growth Efficiency Maintained.]**

He glanced toward the training sword in the corner.

Then he walked past it and flopped back onto the bed.

The TV droned on.

Outside, the gym echoed with shouting, impacts, and desperation.

Inside, Mike closed his eyes.

And grew stronger.
