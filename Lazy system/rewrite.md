# Chapter One

Mike had been training hard for as long as he could remember.

His parents—both normal civilians—had died when he was eight years old. A beast tide had breached the city walls and destroyed the industrial district where they worked. Hundreds were killed before martial artists were finally able to quell the tide.

Since then, Mike had lived in the lowest-grade martial hall, training every day with a single goal: to become a martial artist.

Now he was seventeen.

And still at the **first rank of Martial Apprentice**.

The same rank he had been at when he was eight.

Nine years of relentless training.\
Nine years of effort.\
And not a single step forward.

His body was strong enough. His stance was correct. He understood the basics of sword and fist well enough to teach beginners. In fact, he often did. But no matter how many times he practiced the forms—no matter how carefully he followed the manuals—his power refused to gather properly.

His strikes dispersed instead of condensing.\
His energy slipped through him like water through open fingers.

_Talentless._

That was the word everyone used.

Others his age had already reached the **first stage of Martial Artist**. Even the weakest among them were at least at the **eighth stage of Martial Apprentice**. Those who discovered they could not advance had already accepted their fate and registered as civilians.

The hall allowed Mike to remain only because he was useful.

His foundation was solid. His understanding was clear. He was good at teaching new recruits. But usefulness had limits.

Federal law was absolute.

If he failed to advance to the **second stage of Martial Apprentice** by his eighteenth birthday, he would be registered as a civilian.

No cultivation rights.\
No access to manuals.\
No protection under martial law.

Just another body to be fed into factories, docks, or civil service—a paperwork drone. A life where even speaking back to a martial practitioner could cost him everything.

Another statistic.

Important enough to be kept alive during good times.\
Not important enough to be treated well.

In this world, strength wasn’t just power.

It was permission to exist.

***

The sun had already set by the time Mike left the training hall.

His arms trembled from overuse, his breathing uneven. Today had been no different from the hundreds before it—forms repeated until his muscles screamed, controlled breathing until his lungs burned, focused circulation that went nowhere.

No growth.\
No breakthrough.\
Not even a hint of what he was missing.

The communal showers were empty, steam curling lazily toward the ceiling. Mike stepped under the spray and let the cool water pour over his head, washing away sweat, dust, and frustration. He rested his hands against the tiled wall, forehead pressed forward, eyes closed.

“One month,” he muttered quietly. “I have one month left.”

The students he had taught—students who once looked up to him—had already taken that step.

And yet here he was.

“Why do I train so hard?” he whispered. “Why do I work myself to exhaustion…?”

The thought slipped out before he could stop it.

“Why don’t I just stop? Do the bare minimum. Relax. Enjoy this last month before the inevitable happens.”

A sharp, unfamiliar sound cut through the steady rush of water.

_Ding._

Mike stiffened.

The sound was crisp and mechanical—completely out of place in the quiet shower room.

Before he could react, translucent blue text flickered into existence in front of his eyes.

**\[Initializing System…]**

His breath caught.

The water continued to run. The world didn’t pause. But Mike’s heart began to pound as if it might burst from his chest.

**\[Error detected: Excessive effort usage.]**\
**\[Recalculating growth parameters…]**

Mike blinked hard.

The text didn’t disappear.

“…What?” he whispered.

**\[Lazy System successfully bound.]**

The words lingered, steady and undeniable—as if they had always been meant for him.

Somewhere deep in his chest, something loosened.

Just a little.

***

The blue screen rippled once, then stabilized.

***

**\[Lazy System – Tutorial Mode Initiated]**

**\[Host Detected: Mike Carter]**\
**\[Age: 17]**\
**\[Cultivation Rank: Martial Apprentice – Stage 1 (Unstable)]**\
**\[Evaluation: Excessive Effort / Critically Inefficient]**

Mike swallowed. The assessment was painfully accurate.

“What… is this?” he muttered.

The system did not answer. Instead, new lines appeared—each word precise and merciless.

***

### **\[System Principle]**

> **Effort beyond necessity disrupts natural optimization.**\
> **Forced growth results in energy leakage and stagnation.**\
> **True improvement occurs during non-action.**

Mike frowned.

_Non-action?_

***

### **\[Primary Rule – Lazy Growth]**

> **The less effort the host exerts toward self-improvement, the greater the system’s compensation.**

**Effort Level → Growth Conversion**

* **Complete idleness** → ×100 growth efficiency

* **Casual activity (resting, leisure, observation)** → ×20

* **Normal daily movement** → ×5

* **Intentional training** → ×0.1

* **Forced training / combat under pressure** → ×0.01

* **Obsessive effort detected** → **Growth suppression**

Mike stared at the screen.

This went against everything he had ever known.

Had he pushed himself too hard today? Was he hallucinating now that he was finally about to give up?

***

### **\[Secondary Rule – Passive Mastery]**

> **Understanding requires observation, not repetition.**

* Martial techniques can be comprehended through **passive exposure**

* Flaws, inefficiencies, and redundancies are **automatically highlighted**

* Optimized versions are **integrated without practice**

**Note:**

> _Active rehearsal counts as effort and will reduce gains._

His mouth went dry.

All those years of drilling. All that pain.

If this was true… all he had to do was watch a technique once, and he would understand it—better than its creator.

***

### **\[Tertiary Rule – Flow State]**

> **The system operates only when the host acts without intent to improve.**

* Thinking _“I want to get stronger”_ reduces effectiveness

* Letting go maximizes growth

Mike exhaled slowly.

Letting go.

He had just decided to do exactly that.

And now, apparently, he would be rewarded for it.

***

### **\[Warning]**

> **The Lazy System is exclusive.**\
> **Imitation without compatibility will result in zero gains or backlash.**

Mike almost laughed.

They’d never believe him anyway.
