Let the fun begin a story, power fantasy about a young man who while looking small, malnourished, normal for the poor in this world, who every day grows stronger.  Every minute I get one free attribute point, 1440 a day, I have a SSS level talent, basically Psychokinesis , I can control objects based on my mental power.   so I get 100 free attribute points on each level up also, like the few others who have SSS level talents.  Basically I gain 14 free levels of attributes a Day, in less than a month I have the attributes of the old monsters of level one hundred.  So now the question comes up do I fight for humanity, or do I fight for myself.  I guess this is going to come down to how the powers that be react to me.
Lets start from the beginning, shall we.  My name is Mike, no sur name, at least not in this life.  I was in my early fifties in when I died in my last life.  I thought I had done everything right, I worked hard, saved money, bought land, built a house, was ready to retire and relax, enjoy my silver years.  I was not that lucky, heart attack took me out before I could even begin to enjoy it.

Next thing I remember is waking up in a small cramped box… dark, airless.

At first, I thought the afterlife was crueler than advertised.

I tried to breathe and realized I already was—short, shallow breaths that scraped at my throat. The air smelled like damp earth and old wood. Every movement made the walls creak inches from my face.

A coffin.

The realization landed with a strange calm. Maybe dying once already had taken the edge off panic. Or maybe my mind simply refused to waste energy on fear when it could be gathering information instead.

I reached out.

Not with my hands—those felt weak, thin, too small—but with something else. A pressure behind my eyes. A tug in my thoughts.

And the world answered.

The lid of the coffin didn’t just move.

It _obeyed_.

Wood splintered outward as if struck by an invisible fist. Nails shrieked as they tore free. Dirt cascaded down, cold and heavy, but I pushed again—harder this time—and the soil parted like water before a ship’s bow.

I burst free from the ground under a gray, sickly sky, gasping like a newborn dragged into the world by sheer will.

I lay there for a long moment, chest heaving, mud smeared across skin that was far too thin to be mine.

_Status initialized._

The words appeared without sound, floating at the edge of my vision, clean and emotionless.

***

**Name:** Mike\
**Age:** 16\
**Level:** 1\
**Talent:** SSS-Rank Psychokinesis
**second soul Talent:** Unlimited growth

**Attributes:**

* Strength: 1

* Agility: 1

* Vitality: 1

* Intelligence: 15

* Willpower: 15

**Unassigned Attribute Points:** 1\
**Passive Gain:** +1 Attribute Point / Minute

* **Skills**
  
* Scan - scans an area around the user, radius equal to willpower in meters

***

I stared at the numbers.

Then I laughed. A dry, cracked sound that hurt my throat.

“Of course,” I muttered. “A system.”

It wasn’t rage that bubbled up in my chest—it was _relief_. Cold, sharp, undeniable relief. Because systems had rules. And rules could be exploited.

I focused on the last line.

+1 attribute point per minute.

I did the math automatically. Old habits die hard.

One per minute. Sixty per hour. Fourteen hundred and forty per day.

I swallowed.

That wasn’t growth. That was inevitability.

I pushed myself up onto my elbows, then froze as dizziness washed over me. This body—_my_ body now—was a mess. Ribs visible under pale skin. Arms like sticks. The kind of malnourished frame you only saw in slums or famine zones.

Poor. Forgotten. Disposable.

I had been buried in cheap clothes rags realy, in a shallow grave, without a name worth carving into stone.

So this Mike had died too.

“Guess we have that in common,” I said quietly.

Slowly, carefully, Mike stood.  His legs trembled, but they didn’t give out. He could _feel_ the points stacking up already, one more ticking in, then another. A steady, unstoppable drumbeat.

For the first time since his heart had failed him in his old life, he felt something close to excitement.

Not joy.

Not hope.

_Power._

“Alright,” Mike said to the empty graveyard, voice thin but steady. “Let’s see what kind of world you are.”

He raised his right hand, on the back of it was a pattern, complex and strange, not a tattoo, something that appeared to be part of him.

With a thought stone the size of his head lifted from the ground, hovering effortlessly in the air. No strain. No backlash. Just intent translated directly into reality.

Psychokinesis, SSS-rank.

He lowered the stone gently and looked around, a small clearing in a dark dense forest, a small clean waterfall ending in a small clear pond, a stream heading into the forest, no animal noise, no movement, dead silence, a silence more unnerving than noise ever could be.  Where was he dumped, and who dumped him here. 

This body brought no memories with it, no rules for this world, he will have to figure everything out on his own.

First things first, survival, he was hungry and tired, he need to create a safe space for overnight, don't know if the animals in the woods are hostile, or how strong they are, he has no food, no weapons, and no armor, going to need all three.

Panic wastes calories, indecision wastes time, both get people killed.  "Survival first," Mike said aloud, anchoring his thoughts.  The sound of his own voice was thin, but it was real. "Shelter, water, food.  In that order."

Mike looking around noticed the world seemed odd, the sky a blue grey, no sun, light seemed to come from everywhere, no noticeable shadows, a complete lack of wildlife, no fish, no bugs, no birds.  It was all too odd, a bit fake, like bing in a game.  

Mike focused his mind and activated the Scan skill, information washed over his mind, not words, not images, but _awareness_.  A sphere expanded outward from him, fifteen meters n every direction mapped cleanly by the skill.   No animals, no birds, no insects, no fish, no worms.  That is not normal.  

Good news though, there are plants that are edible according to the skill, some carrots, some potatoes, some onions.  At least there is some food here.  He will not be starving tonight, assuming there is a night.  Need to learn the day night cycle of wherever he is.

Mike looks around, shelter first.  Mike focuses on a small tree with large leaves, he thinks of an ax chopping the tree and sure enough a mark forms in the bark, a health bar pops op in front of the tree, 80%, with a couple of more strikes the tree falls, the sound of creaking wood filling the forest.  Just when the tree should hit the ground something odd happens, it simply disappears. 

> **logs 10 added to inventory**
> **leaves 40 added to inventory**
> **inventory weight limit reached, please level up and increase strength to increase inventory weight limit, movement speed reduced until items are removed from inventory**

Mike froze mid-thought.  "Inventory?  So this is a game type world, this must be some type of tutorial zone."  He focused inward the same way he had with Scan and a translucent grid unfolded in his mind's eye, two slots, clean, simple.  one with a pixelated picture of a stack of logs, a simple 10 written over it, the other a picture of a pile of leaves, a simple 40 written over it.  Nice this should make things easier he thought.  Until he tried to take a step.

Slowly he moved his right foot out to where it was going to plant, then his left foot, painfully slowly he moved.  Now he began to understand the reduced movement speed, he is moving at most 25 percent the speed that he had been walking just moments before.  Thankfully he did not have to move far.  With a thought he removed the logs and leaves from the inventory and a neat stack of ten logs and a large pile of leaves appeared on the ground next to the cliff face.
