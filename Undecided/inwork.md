Mike didn’t linger.

That, more than the boss kill itself, would have confused anyone watching—if anyone _could_ see him.

He slipped the glowing book and scrolls deeper into his inventory, mentally tagging them as _future problems_. The knives, though… those were immediate.

One hundred slim, black blades appeared around him in a loose halo, each humming faintly as his will brushed against them. They were light—almost weightless to his senses—and the moment he stopped feeding power into them, they stilled obediently, hovering just behind his shoulders.

“Automatic return,” Mike murmured. “Good. Ammo problems are for idiots.”

He took one last look at the clearing where the Gravewarden Wolf had fallen. Already, the ground was smoothing itself out. Broken trees faded into motes of light. Even the trench carved by the wolf’s body was filling in.

The tutorial didn’t like scars.

Mike turned his back on the center of the realm and began walking.

***

### Edge of the Center Zone

The beasts here were different.

Smaller. Quieter. Level indicators flickered faintly in his awareness as Scan rolled outward in a constant pulse.

> **Forest Hare – Level 2**\
> **Thornback Lizard – Level 3**\
> **Stonejaw Boar – Level 5**

Perfect.

Mike lifted one finger.

A single knife vanished from the halo and reappeared thirty meters away, already embedded cleanly through a boar’s skull.

The body dropped.

> **Kill confirmed.**\
> **EXP gained.**

The knife snapped back into formation without a sound.

Mike didn’t smile—but something in his posture loosened.

“So,” he said quietly, walking forward, “the system still works.”

He didn’t rush. Didn’t chase. He let the beasts come into his Scan, flicked knives, gathered drops, moved on. Efficient. Calm. Almost boring.

Attribute points continued to stack.

One per minute.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

He didn’t spend them.

Not yet.

***

### Far Behind Him – Watching Eyes

They didn’t see him fight.

They saw _aftermath_.

A snapped branch here. A bloodstain already fading there. Empty spawn zones where beasts should have been roaming.

Jason crouched low, hand raised to stop his group.

“…Something’s clearing mobs ahead of us.”

Mara frowned. “A party?”

Jason shook his head. “Too clean.”

They advanced carefully, tension thickening with every step.

Then they saw it.

A boy.

Thin. Pale. Clothes little better than rags. Barefoot, moving calmly through the forest like he belonged there.

No armor.

No visible weapon.

He flicked his hand lazily.

A boar fell over dead thirty meters away.

The knife returned to him so fast it was almost invisible.

Silence.

“…That’s him,” someone whispered.

Mara swallowed. “He looks… normal.”

Jason’s jaw clenched.

That made it worse.

***

### Mike

He felt them before he saw them.

Scan brushed against unfamiliar minds—nervous, clustered, watching.

He didn’t turn.

Didn’t acknowledge them.

Instead, he reached into his inventory and pulled out a piece of wolf meat, skewered it on a stick, and held it over a small fire he’d started with a thought and a spark of friction.

Calm.

Deliberate.

A statement.

_I know you’re there.\
I don’t care._

The watchers didn’t move closer.

Not yet.

***

### Elsewhere – The Realm Observers

Data streamed endlessly.

Kill counts.\
Growth curves.\
Talent interference.

One graph dwarfed the rest, climbing at a steady, absurd angle.

“…He’s not optimizing for EXP,” one observer noted.

“No,” another agreed. “He’s optimizing for _time_.”

Silence followed.

“That’s worse.”

***

### Back in the Forest

Mike chewed thoughtfully and watched the sky cycle slowly toward dusk.

Level 2 came quietly.

> **Level Up!**\
> **Unassigned Attribute Points: +100**

He felt no rush of excitement.

Just confirmation.

Twenty to thirty days for everyone else.

For him?

Mike looked at the steadily ticking passive counter.

“…We’ll see who gets impatient first.”

He stood, knives swirling into formation once more, and walked deeper into the forest—away from safety, away from crowds, away from expectations.

Behind him, rumors sharpened into plans.

Ahead of him, power waited.

And somewhere above it all, the system watched a boy who grew stronger every minute… and quietly recalculated what “human” was supposed to mean.
